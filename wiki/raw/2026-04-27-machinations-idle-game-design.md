## What is an idle game?

Idle games (incremental games, clicker games) — games that continue to progress without any input from the user. Player can actively interact or let it run in the background.

From a game design POV: idle games must accommodate both active play and idle progression.

## Why are idle games so popular?

- Sense of continued accomplishment — progress without constant interaction
- Trophies, badges, achievements create clear sense of achievement
- Optimization before idle mode makes downtime feel like strategic play
- "People like games in which the numbers go up" — purest version of that mechanic

## What makes a well-designed idle game?

Three common factors:

1. **Easy, low barrier to entry core loop** — player clicks/taps → earns reward (in-game currency/resource)
2. **Sophisticated game economy design** — reason to spend currency and return to core loop; purchased items increase currency per click
3. **Achievement counter** — visible counter showing resources generated per minute/hour cycle

These three factors establish a core loop, but it has a finite draw. To keep players engaged, core loop must interact with a meta loop.

## What is a meta loop?

Meta loop is what keeps players playing beyond the thrill of watching numbers go up. Can be surprisingly complex.

Example: RPG character advancement with primary + secondary currency model:
- Primary currency: heroes auto-combat enemies, collect primary currency → buy basic upgrades (increase attack power + currency rewards). Can be automated.
- Secondary currency: appears far less frequently (end of stages, boss defeats). Used for upgrades beyond basic gear. Always a pinch on this currency to encourage real-world purchases.
- Teamwork aspect: certain heroes + advancements work together for better results.
- New heroes introduced over time → add options to meta loop.

## How to design an idle game

### Game balance
Critical — how easily player accumulates currencies/resources. Balance between enough resources to keep engaging + scarcity to tempt real money purchases.

### Difficulty and complexity
Core loop starts and continues very simple. Offset by meta loop that is surprisingly complex with secondary difficulty in managing meta mechanics.

### Rewards
Player must feel rewarded for both active and idle time. Too few → no impetus to continue. Too many → resources devalued, diminished impact.

### Having a good idle mode
Robust offline mode: player progresses when not interacting. Always return to find reward earned.

### Having a good active mode
Active gameplay must be engaging beyond "log in, spend currency, log out."

### A 'return reward'
Large amount of basic currency when returning — reward in itself, keeps players motivated to actively participate.

### Meaningful choices and rewards
Meta loop needs genuinely meaningful choices — gear and upgrade choices that make player feel their actions matter, skills and strategies impact the game.

## 5 Tips for designing idle games

1. **Always invest in strong theming** — simple mechanics = theming and art attract customers. Stand out from thousands of idle games.
2. **Soft launch your game** — fine-tune balance, ad placement, in-game economy in limited territories before global launch.
3. **Respond to feedback** — player feedback helps fine-tune; responding turns players into evangelists.
4. **Create a complex meta loop** — don't rely on one mechanic. Multiple interacting mechanics introduced gradually, forcing strategy changes.
5. **Don't prioritize monetization over retention** — long-tail earnings > immediate monetization. Overt monetization drives players away.
